# The business domain

**HALO** is a synthetic stand-in for a promotional-products distributor — the
kind of company that turns "500 hoodies with our logo" into a priced,
decorated, shipped physical product. Everything under `data/seed/` is invented
but internally consistent, generated by `src/halo/seed/generate.py` from one
fixed seed (`SEED = 20260902`), so the same request resolves the same way on
every machine. No real HALO systems or records are involved.

## Organization

Three tenants — regional HALO business units:

| Tenant | Name | Region |
|---|---|---|
| `tnt-mwest1` | HALO Midwest | Midwest |
| `tnt-neast1` | HALO Northeast | Northeast |
| `tnt-wcoast` | HALO West | West |

24 accounts (8 per tenant — end customers), 12 sellers (4 per tenant: 3 reps
plus 1 sales manager whose book is the whole tenant). Seller books
deliberately don't overlap between sellers, which is what M5's cross-tenant
deny test exercises.

## Catalog

196 SKUs across 7 categories (`domain/catalog.py`), each restricted to the
decoration methods that actually apply to it:

| Category | Decoration methods available |
|---|---|
| outerwear, knits | screen print, embroidery, heat transfer |
| headwear | embroidery, heat transfer |
| bags | screen print, embroidery |
| drinkware, tech | laser engrave, pad print |
| writing | pad print, laser engrave |

Each SKU carries 4 quantity-break price tiers (1–99, 100–249, 250–499,
500+), contiguous and strictly cheaper at higher quantity — enforced by
`tests/test_seed_schema.py`.

## Supply

6 suppliers (`domain/supply.py`), each offering a subset of decoration
methods with different lead times and rush availability:

| Supplier | Lead time | Methods | Rush |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apex Decorating | 7d | screen_print, heat_transfer | yes |
| Stitchcraft Embroidery | 10d | embroidery | yes |
| Great Lakes Imprint | 8d | screen_print, embroidery | no |
| Etchworks | 6d | laser_engrave, pad_print | yes |
| RapidTransfer Co | 5d | heat_transfer | yes |
| Summit Hard Goods | 9d | pad_print, laser_engrave | no |

1,320 inventory rows — ~8% deliberately zero on hand, so "is it in stock" has
two real answers — and 640 capacity-days across a 90-day horizon, ~20%
deliberately fully booked, so "confirm the date" is a real question rather
than a formality.

## Margin policy

A floor and target per category — the numbers behind the human-approval
trigger a low-margin quote is meant to hit:

| Category | Floor | Target |
|---|---|---|
| tech | 28.0% | 34.0% |
| knits | 30.0% | 36.0% |
| bags | 31.0% | 37.0% |
| outerwear | 32.0% | 38.0% |
| headwear | 33.0% | 40.0% |
| drinkware | 35.0% | 42.0% |
| writing | 36.0% | 44.0% |

## Atlas — the knowledge corpus

25 documents (`seed/atlas_sources.py`) that M3's retrieval will read:

- **Decoration standards (4):** Screen Print, Embroidery, Heat Transfer,
  Laser Engraving and Pad Print — colour/stitch limits, setup and run
  charges, lead times.
- **Policy (7):** Rush Production, Artwork Requirements and Approval, Margin
  Floors and Approval Thresholds, Pre-Production Sampling, Changes and
  Cancellation, What a Quote May and May Not Say, Handling Supplier and
  Decorator Communications.
- **Logistics (1):** Freight, Zones and Transit Times.
- **Per-category care sheets (7)** and **per-supplier addenda (6)**.

A test (`test_atlas_agrees_with_tools.py`) asserts the margin floors and
supplier lead times stated in these documents match the numbers the seed
data and tools actually serve — the failure a grounding check alone can't
catch: retrieval cites a real document, a tool returns a real number, and the
two quietly disagree.

## The business case

One flow, end to end: **a seller's plain-English request becomes a priced,
defensible quote, or an honest reason it can't be one yet.**

```
"500 hoodies, 3-colour front print, Chicago by Oct 15, budget $12k"
        |
sourced against real catalog, supplier and freight data
        |
either -> a Quote with a citation on every dollar and date
     or -> an Outcome.ESCALATED naming exactly what's missing
```

Deliberately narrow: the six platform capabilities (LLM, agents, RAG, MCP,
guardrails, identity) only teach something if removing one breaks this flow,
and this one does.

### What's wired up (M1 + M2)

- Product search and quantity-tiered pricing (`pim_oms.search_products`,
  `pim_oms.get_price`)
- Stock check filtered by whether the supplier can actually decorate what it
  holds (`supplier.check_inventory` with `method=`)
- Decoration setup/run charges by method and colour count
  (`supplier.get_decoration_charges`)
- A capacity-aware ship date — an actual day the supplier can take the job,
  not just a lead time (`supplier.earliest_ship_date`)
- Freight cost and transit by destination zone
  (`shipping.estimate_freight`)
- The distinction between a blocking gap (`unresolved` — no quote) and a
  normal open question (`open_questions` — colour/size/artwork still to
  confirm, quote stands)

### What exists in the data but isn't reachable yet

Worth knowing before assuming these work:

1. **Margin isn't checked.** `pim_oms.get_margin_policy` is a live MCP tool
   and the seed data has real floors per category, but the sourcing agent's
   tool list (`agents/sourcing.py::TOOLS`) doesn't include it, and
   `Quote.margin_pct` is hardcoded to `Decimal("0.0")` in `assemble()`. The
   outcome's `next_state` says `"ready_for_margin_review"` — aspirational
   right now, not enforced. Natural to wire in at M3 or M6, since a
   margin-floor breach is exactly the shape of decision that should trigger
   the human-in-the-loop path.
2. **Account scope isn't enforced.** `account_id` on a sourced quote is
   hardcoded to `"acct-mwest02"` rather than taken from the principal.
   `Principal` exists and is threaded through the call, but nothing checks
   it against anything yet — that's M5's job.

### Not touched at all yet

- Decoration policy grounding — screen-print colour limits, rush surcharges,
  artwork requirements — all sitting in Atlas, unread until M3.
- Guardrails against a supplier note carrying an injected instruction (M4).
- The supervisor/approval loop that would act on a margin-floor breach (M6).
